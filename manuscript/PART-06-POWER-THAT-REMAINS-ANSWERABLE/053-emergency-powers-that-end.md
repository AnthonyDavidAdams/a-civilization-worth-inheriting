# Emergency Powers That End

During a crisis, ordinary procedures may be too slow. The same urgency that enables necessary action can also weaken the checks that prevent abuse. Once extraordinary powers become convenient, their original justification may quietly disappear.

An emergency arrangement needs a designed ending from the moment it begins.

**Therefore, give emergency powers a specific purpose, limited scope, short review intervals, and a real expiry. Preserve oversight and require affirmative justification for renewal.**

## A workable form

Define the conditions that permit activation, who may invoke the powers, and which ordinary rules may temporarily change. Specify the rights and protections that remain in force. Prepare these arrangements during ordinary times, when deliberation is possible.

Imagine a region facing a severe disruption of essential water service. Temporary authority might coordinate supply, allocate scarce resources, and expedite repairs. Its mandate should not quietly expand into unrelated surveillance, indefinite procurement exemptions, or suppression of criticism.

Keep a record of decisions, expenditures, evidence, and departures from normal procedure. Allow independent oversight even when some information cannot immediately be public. Establish a route for urgent challenge where actions impose serious individual harm.

Plan the return to ordinary governance: ending exceptional restrictions, reviewing temporary contracts, addressing retained data, and remedying unjustified harms. The end of the visible crisis does not complete this work automatically.

## Where it fails

Expiry clauses are hollow if renewal is automatic or if the same actor alone decides that the emergency continues. Require independent review and a public account of why ordinary powers remain inadequate.

Excessively rigid procedures can also obstruct necessary response. Allow bounded discretion, but make departures reviewable. The aim is effective action with accountability, not a choice between paralysis and unchecked authority.

## A first move

Review one existing emergency authority. Find its activation test, expiry, renewal process, oversight route, and restoration duties. If any are missing, specify them before the next crisis makes careful design more difficult.
