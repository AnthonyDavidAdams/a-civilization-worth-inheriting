# The Right to Exit and Belong

A community becomes coercive when leaving it means losing housing, livelihood, children, identity, or every trusted relationship. Yet a society composed only of easy exits can leave people without durable commitments or places that feel like home.

Freedom requires both the ability to leave harmful arrangements and the possibility of belonging without submission.

**Therefore, make membership meaningful and departure possible. Reduce avoidable dependence on a single employer, household, institution, platform, or political community, while protecting continuity of essential rights and relationships.**

## A workable form

Examine the cost of refusal. Can someone change jobs without losing necessary care? Leave a household without becoming homeless? Depart a digital service without losing their records? Challenge a community leader without forfeiting every source of support?

Imagine a residential association that combines housing, work, education, and social life. Its cohesion may be valuable, but its members need independent access to information, outside relationships, clear financial terms, and practical assistance if they choose to leave.

Portability helps where dependence accumulates: credentials, savings, essential records, and benefits should travel when feasible. So do independent institutions that can assist people without requiring permission from the organization they wish to leave.

Belonging also needs protection. People should not be expelled arbitrarily because they dissent, become disabled, or cease to be useful. Establish fair membership rules, proportional responses to conflict, and a process that hears the person at risk of exclusion.

## Where it fails

Formal permission to leave can hide impossible practical costs. Evaluate what departure actually requires of someone with little money, limited mobility, or dependent children.

Exit can also transfer burdens to those remaining. A powerful member may depart after extracting benefits and leave debts behind. Preserve fair obligations without using them as a pretext for captivity.

## A first move

Choose one institution central to your life and describe the steps needed to leave it. Identify which losses are unavoidable and which are designed into the arrangement. Change one unnecessary dependency while maintaining the relationships people freely value.
