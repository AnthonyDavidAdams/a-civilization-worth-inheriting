# Peace as a Maintained Practice

Peace is visible when violence is absent, but much of its work happens before a confrontation: channels that remain open, grievances that receive a hearing, institutions that limit humiliation, and people who can interrupt escalation without losing face.

A society that budgets for conflict while neglecting these capacities leaves prevention to luck.

**Therefore, maintain the relationships, institutions, and material conditions that make nonviolent resolution possible. Protect people from coercion while creating credible routes away from recurring conflict.**

## A workable form

Build ordinary capacity for mediation, fair adjudication, and early attention to grievances. Make these services available before disputes become emergencies. Include people trusted across divisions without assuming that a respected intermediary is neutral in every matter.

Imagine two communities competing over seasonal water. A standing forum reviews conditions, hears claims, and activates agreed scarcity arrangements. Participants know how to contact one another during an incident and how to correct a rumor before it becomes an accusation.

Peace requires justice as well as conversation. Address exclusion, dispossession, and recurring abuse. Protect those who speak about harm; do not demand reconciliation as the price of being heard.

At larger scales, sustain communication, verifiable agreements, civilian protection, and mechanisms that reduce dangerous misinterpretation. Necessary defensive capacity should remain accountable and proportionate. Its existence does not remove the obligation to seek durable resolution.

## Where it fails

Calls for calm can favor those benefiting from the current arrangement. Ask whose suffering is being made invisible by the appearance of order.

Dialogue can also expose vulnerable people to manipulation or retaliation. Participation should be supported and, where appropriate, voluntary. A process must be able to recognize bad faith and protect participants while leaving room for genuine change.

## A first move

Identify one recurring conflict before its next predictable escalation. Establish a trusted channel, agree on how disputed facts will be checked, and define an immediate protective step for anyone at risk. Evaluate whether the arrangement reduces harm and makes grievances more answerable.
