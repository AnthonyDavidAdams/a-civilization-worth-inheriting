# Power Must Explain Itself

The application is refused. The letter cites policy, the office cites the system, and the system has no address. Power has acted, but nobody appears responsible for the action.

An explanation matters because it makes a decision possible to understand, contest, and improve. It also reveals whether a legitimate reason existed in the first place.

**Therefore, require consequential power to give intelligible reasons, identify accountable decision-makers, and provide a practical route to challenge and remedy. Scale the obligation to the effects of the decision.**

## A workable form

Explain what was decided, which information mattered, which rules applied, and what alternatives were considered. State uncertainty and identify information that could change the outcome. A list of technical inputs is not necessarily an explanation a person can use.

Imagine a public agency allocating scarce housing. Applicants should understand eligibility, priority, the evidence used in their case, and how to correct an error. The public should also understand the broader allocation policy and its distributional effects without exposing applicants' private lives.

Provide review by someone with sufficient independence and authority to change the result. Make the process affordable, accessible, and timely enough to matter. A successful appeal after a person has lost their home may offer an inadequate remedy.

Apply the principle to private institutions where their practical power is substantial. Essential employers, landlords, platforms, and service providers can shape lives as decisively as public offices. Accountability should follow dependence and consequence.

## Where it fails

Disclosure can overwhelm rather than clarify. Thousands of pages may conceal the decisive assumption. Offer a concise account with routes to deeper evidence and independent examination.

Some information deserves protection, including personal privacy and details whose disclosure would create concrete danger. Use narrowly defined exceptions with oversight. Secrecy should not erase the duty to justify the decision to an appropriate reviewer.

## A first move

Find one consequential decision people routinely struggle to understand. Rewrite its explanation with affected users, add a named route for correction, and test whether a person can identify both the reason and their next available step.
