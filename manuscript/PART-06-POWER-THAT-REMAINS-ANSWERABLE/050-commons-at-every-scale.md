# Commons at Every Scale

A shared garden, a fishery, a body of knowledge, and an atmosphere all invite common care. They do not invite identical rules. The users, boundaries, consequences, and possibilities of exclusion differ.

A commons is sustained through relationships and institutions, not through the word shared alone.

**Therefore, create governance suited to each shared resource, with clear responsibilities, meaningful participation, monitoring, and fair ways to resolve conflict. Connect local arrangements to wider systems where consequences cross boundaries.**

## A workable form

Identify the resource, its users, those affected by its use, and the conditions of its renewal. Ask which decisions can be made locally and which require coordination beyond the immediate group.

Imagine several communities drawing from connected water sources. Each can organize local use, but none can responsibly ignore its effects downstream. A wider agreement sets shared limits and procedures while allowing local adaptation within them.

Ostrom's work on polycentric governance challenges the assumption that every collective problem has one institutional solution. It directs attention to arrangements that fit circumstances and to interacting centers of decision-making. The specific design still requires investigation and revision. [S02]

Make duties visible and enforceable. Members need ways to observe condition, raise concerns, address violations proportionately, and revise rules. Include newcomers and people affected without being formal members. A successful group can otherwise preserve its resource by exporting costs.

## Where it fails

Common ownership can coexist with domination by a few confident participants. Rotate appropriate roles, disclose decisions, compensate demanding work, and make it possible to challenge leaders.

Local autonomy can also become a shield against wider obligations. Where damage travels, governance must travel far enough to address it. Coordination should support local knowledge while preventing any locality from claiming unlimited freedom to harm others.

## A first move

Choose one resource already shared in practice. Write down its users, caretakers, rules, and unresolved conflicts. Address the largest gap between who benefits, who decides, and who performs the work of keeping it available.
