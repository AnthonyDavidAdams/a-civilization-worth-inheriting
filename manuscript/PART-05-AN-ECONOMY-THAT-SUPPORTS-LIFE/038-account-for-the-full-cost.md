# Account for the Full Cost

A cheap product may arrive with an expensive absence: the repair nobody budgets for, the illness borne elsewhere, the exhausted landscape omitted from the invoice. A transaction can be voluntary for its participants while imposing involuntary costs on everyone outside it.

The future is especially easy to exclude. It cannot refuse delivery of the waste.

**Therefore, make consequential costs visible and assign responsibility to those able to prevent or bear them fairly. Use prices where they help, and enforce limits where payment cannot justify the harm.**

## A workable form

Follow an activity through extraction, production, use, maintenance, and retirement. Ask who supplies unpaid labor, who encounters pollution, and which obligations will remain after the seller has gone. Identify uncertainties rather than giving every unknown a conveniently precise price.

Imagine a municipality choosing paving materials. Purchase price matters, but so do installation, heat, drainage, repair, worker conditions, and eventual removal. Compare plausible lifetime consequences and record assumptions that could change the choice.


Different harms require different instruments. Some can be reduced through charges, deposits, procurement requirements, or responsibility for take-back. Others require prohibitions, protection of rights, or limits that cannot be purchased away. Compensation does not recreate an extinct species or automatically repair a violated relationship.

When higher prices would deprive people of essentials, protect access while changing the harmful system. Support households and workers through the transition. Otherwise the apparent efficiency of charging for damage can transfer the burden to those with the fewest alternatives.

## Where it fails

Full-cost accounting can promise a completeness it cannot deliver. Models embed judgments about boundaries, valuation, and time. Publish those judgments and show how conclusions change under reasonable alternatives.

It can also turn moral questions into shopping decisions. A wealthy actor may treat a fee as permission to continue causing harm. Pair accounting with enforceable duties, independent scrutiny, and the ability to say no.

## A first move

Choose one recurring purchase and list the people, places, and future obligations missing from its price. Address the largest credible omission in the next contract, and specify how you will check whether responsibility actually changed.
