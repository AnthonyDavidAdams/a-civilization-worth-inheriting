# Share the Gains of Invention

A new machine makes a week's work possible in a day. One possible future gives everyone more security and time. Another dismisses most workers, intensifies the work of those remaining, and concentrates the proceeds. The invention alone does not choose between them.

Productivity is a technical achievement whose benefits depend on institutions.

**Therefore, establish fair ways to share the gains of invention before those gains become concentrated. Include the people whose labor, knowledge, public support, and inherited resources make the advance possible.**

## A workable form

Begin by mapping contributions and risks. An innovation may depend on years of public research, practical knowledge from workers, infrastructure, users' participation, and private investment. Recognizing these contributions does not require pretending that they are identical.

Imagine a cooperative introducing an automated production process. Workers examine the expected savings, the uncertainty, and the remaining tasks. They negotiate a combination of reduced hours, improved services, training, reserves, and compensation. Decisions can change as the actual gains become visible.

Other settings will need other arrangements: profit sharing, public returns on publicly supported inventions, wider ownership, competitive access, or taxation that funds common services. Evaluate outcomes rather than assuming any ownership label guarantees fairness.

Include people outside the successful organization. A firm may share generously with its own employees while undermining suppliers or excluding an entire region from new opportunities. Transition arrangements should consider displaced livelihoods and the social infrastructure attached to them.

## Where it fails

Promises of future abundance can excuse present dispossession. Set obligations early, when access to funding, procurement, or permission provides an opportunity to negotiate them.

Sharing can also discourage useful risk if it ignores genuine costs and effort. Make the bargain intelligible: reward contribution and initiative while resisting claims to unlimited control over benefits built upon a much wider inheritance.

## A first move

Before adopting one labor-saving tool, write down who is expected to gain, who may lose income or bargaining power, and how the gains will be measured. Agree on a review after deployment, with affected workers able to influence the response.
