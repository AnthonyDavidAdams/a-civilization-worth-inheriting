# Earth Is Not Disposable

Imagine a child watching the lights of an orbital settlement cross the evening sky. Expansion has made the universe feel more accessible. Below the child, the soil, water, atmosphere, and innumerable relationships of Earth still sustain the life doing the imagining.

A future beyond Earth should deepen responsibility for our first world rather than offer a story in which abandoning it becomes acceptable.

**Therefore, pursue exploration and possible settlement beyond Earth while treating the continuity of Earth's living systems as a central obligation. Evaluate new capabilities by how they affect that obligation and the people who remain here.**

## A workable form

Assess space projects alongside their terrestrial requirements and consequences: energy, materials, labor, environmental effects, public support, and competing needs. Benefits such as knowledge, observation, and useful technology deserve consideration without being assumed to justify every project.

Imagine a future settlement program whose charter includes responsibilities to Earth. It funds stewardship of its own material footprint, shares appropriate knowledge, and avoids presenting migration as a substitute for addressing damage. Participation in exploration does not erase obligations arising from activities at home.

Keep access and distribution visible. If only a few can leave a deteriorating place, the promise of escape may weaken their incentive to protect everyone else. Institutions should connect the benefits of expansion to continuing common responsibilities.

Preserve the emotional relationship as well. Education and culture can hold exploration together with attention to ordinary life: a shoreline, a migrating bird, a garden, a neighborhood. Wonder at distant worlds need not diminish wonder at this one.

## Where it fails

Earth-first language can become a blanket rejection of inquiry or a demand that no exploration occur until every other problem is solved. Evaluate specific projects and opportunity costs rather than imposing an impossible condition of universal completion.

The opposite failure is to treat hypothetical future abundance as permission for present damage. Promised substitutes should not be counted as established capabilities.

## A first move

For one proposed expansion or exploration project, write an account of its Earthbound obligations and plausible benefits. Identify which promises depend on uncertain future capabilities. Judge the present commitment using what can responsibly be supported now.
