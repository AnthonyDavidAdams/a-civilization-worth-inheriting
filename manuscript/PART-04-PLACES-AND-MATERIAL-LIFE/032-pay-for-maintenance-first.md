# Pay for Maintenance First

Opening ceremonies are easier to celebrate than a repaired roof. New assets can attract attention while the work required to sustain old ones remains an annual negotiation. Eventually a community owns more than it can responsibly keep.

Maintenance is a promise made to everyone who will depend on an asset. The promise should be visible when the asset is proposed, including the possibility that retiring it will be the responsible choice.

**Therefore, establish credible provision for operation, maintenance, replacement, and eventual retirement before adding an asset or service. Give ongoing care a stable place in budgets and public recognition.**

## A workable form

Imagine a town offered money for a new recreation building. The gift covers construction but leaves utilities, staff, repairs, and future replacement to the town. Before accepting, compare those obligations with existing commitments and likely resources.

Prepare an understandable lifecycle plan with uncertainty shown. Identify who inspects the building, who can authorize repairs, what reserves or funding streams support the work, and how decisions will change if use declines. An endowment is one possible arrangement; its adequacy and governance still require scrutiny.

Give maintenance workers influence over design. They can identify inaccessible components, fragile finishes, and tasks whose apparent simplicity conceals recurring costs. Include their knowledge while changes remain inexpensive.

Make deferred work visible as an obligation rather than a saving. Distinguish essential preventive work from improvements that can reasonably wait. When resources are insufficient, publish the consequences and consider reducing the asset base instead of allowing silent deterioration.

## Where it fails

Maintenance budgets can become protected expenditures that nobody examines. Stable provision should coexist with transparent priorities and review of whether the service remains useful.

The pattern can also be misused to block necessary investment by demanding certainty about distant costs. Require a credible plan and capacity to adapt, not impossible foresight. Compare the proposed asset with the costs of continuing without it.

## A first move

List the essential assets in one organization and identify who is responsible for their next major repair. Where responsibility or funding is missing, address that gap before approving an expansion that adds another obligation.
