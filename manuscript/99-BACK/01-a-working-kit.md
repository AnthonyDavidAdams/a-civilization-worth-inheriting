# A working kit

## The pattern worksheet

Use one worksheet for a consequential decision, then revise it as the work changes. Keep the answers short enough that participants can read and challenge them.

1. **Place and purpose.** Where are we working, and what should become better for whom?
2. **Current conditions.** What is already happening? What do we know through observation, and what are we assuming?
3. **People and other lives affected.** Who benefits, who bears burdens, and who is missing from the decision? Who represents absent interests, and how are those representatives accountable?
4. **Selected patterns.** Which three to seven patterns matter most? What does each require here?
5. **Tensions and alternatives.** Where do the patterns pull in different directions? What are at least two feasible responses, including the consequences of doing nothing?
6. **Authority and consent.** Who can decide, refuse, appeal, pause, or require a change? Which permissions are needed before beginning?
7. **Resources and care.** Who will do the work, pay the costs, maintain the result, and inherit unfinished duties?
8. **Trial and boundaries.** What can we learn through a limited first step? What would cause us to stop? How will we end responsibly?
9. **Evidence and review.** What would count as improvement or harm? Who will examine the result independently, and when?
10. **Public memory.** What should be shared, what should remain private, and where will the account be kept for successors?

## Starter combinations

**A household with more care than time.** Begin with Care Across a Lifetime (16), Pay for Care (43), and Time That Is Your Own (91). Add The Skill of Relationship (12) and Consent as a Daily Practice (13) so practical support remains connected to relationships and consent. Ask whose responsibility currently has no dependable relief.

**A neighborhood gathering place.** Combine A Common Table (14), Beauty in Ordinary Places (33), Public Space Without a Purchase (34), Hospitality Across Difference (96), and Pay for Maintenance First (32). Add A Place to Be Alone (15) where people need quiet alongside sociability. Begin with access and continuing care before choosing a visual identity.

**An organization adopting AI.** Combine Delegated Intelligence (64), Auditable Power (65), Capability With Containment (66), Human Skills Worth Keeping (68), and No Irreversible Surrender (72). Add Share the Gains of Invention (39) and A Fair Transition (45) where work or livelihoods will change. Define the mandate and the route to correction before expanding authority.

**A watershed under pressure.** Combine Watersheds as Commons (19), Connected Habitats (21), Soil as an Inheritance (24), Guardians With Duties (27), and Commons at Every Scale (50). Add Account for the Full Cost (38) and Map Who Bears the Cost (101) to reveal burdens carried beyond the immediate users. Work across the relevant boundaries rather than assuming one jurisdiction contains the problem.

**A transition away from a harmful industry.** Combine Work With Meaning (17), Account for the Full Cost (38), Share the Gains of Invention (39), A Fair Transition (45), and Learning Across a Lifetime (58). Add Memory of Harm (62) where past harm still shapes trust. A credible plan should describe the next life of the community as well as the end of an activity.

**An institution preparing for disruption.** Combine Diverse Systems (73), Failure That Stays Local (74), Reserves Before Scarcity (76), Practice the Emergency (77), and Recovery Before Disaster (78). Add Libraries That Survive (59), A Civilization That Can Reboot (79), and Institutions With Successors (80) for continuity of knowledge and responsibility. Test the weakest dependency before buying more equipment.

## A first month

**Week one: learn the place.** Observe, listen, and write a short account others can correct. Choose a question small enough to investigate and important enough to matter.

**Week two: compose the patterns.** Select a few relevant chapters, map benefits and burdens, and develop two plausible responses. Find the person or interest most likely to be overlooked.

**Week three: prepare a bounded action.** Establish resources, permissions, responsibilities, a review date, and an ending plan. Begin a small trial only where the stakes and conditions make that appropriate.

**Week four: examine and record.** Look for unintended effects and ask an independent person to challenge the account. Decide whether to continue, change, stop, or gather more evidence. Share what others can use.
