# Sources and further reading

The bracketed codes identify the sources explicitly discussed in the chapters. Publication dates distinguish the cited work from later developments. Online sources were consulted in September 2026.

## S01 — The pattern-language form

Christopher Alexander, Sara Ishikawa, and Murray Silverstein, with Max Jacobson, Ingrid Fiksdahl-King, and Shlomo Angel. *A Pattern Language: Towns, Buildings, Construction*. Oxford University Press, 1977. The formal inspiration for linked responses to recurring design problems; the civilization patterns in this book are separate proposals.

Official book page (<https://www.patternlanguage.com/bookstore/pattern-language.html>). Online introduction and sampler (<https://www.patternlanguage.com/apl/aplsample/aplsampleintroduction.htm>).

## S02 — Commons and polycentric governance

Elinor Ostrom. *Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems*. Nobel Prize Lecture, 2009. A foundation for examining multiple, interacting centers of governance and institutions suited to particular collective problems.

Lecture and materials (<https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/ostrom/lecture/>).

## S03 — Early childhood and nurturing care

World Health Organization, United Nations Children's Fund, and World Bank Group. *Nurturing Care for Early Childhood Development: A Framework for Helping Children Survive and Thrive to Transform Health and Human Potential*. 2018.

Framework (<https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241514064>).

## S04 — Human rights

United Nations General Assembly. *Universal Declaration of Human Rights*. 1948. A normative reference for equal dignity and basic protections; the book's institutional proposals should be assessed separately from the declaration itself.

Declaration (<https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights>).

## S05 — Climate and development

Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change. *Climate Change 2023: Synthesis Report*. 2023, especially the Summary for Policymakers. Used for the connection among climate response, development, equity, and governance, and for the need for substantial emissions reductions.

Report (<https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/>).

## S06 — Biodiversity

Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services. *Global Assessment Report on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services: Summary for Policymakers*. 2019.

Summary for policymakers (<https://files.ipbes.net/ipbes-web-prod-public-files/inline/files/ipbes_global_assessment_report_summary_for_policymakers.pdf>).

## S07 — Animal consciousness

*The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness*. 2024. A statement by signatories about the evidential basis for considering conscious experience in other animals, with accompanying background. It does not establish identical experiences across species.

Declaration (<https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration>). Background (<https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/background>).

## S08 — Representative deliberation

Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development. *Innovative Citizen Participation and New Democratic Institutions: Catching the Deliberative Wave*. 2020. Comparative material on representative deliberative processes and their design.

Report (<https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/innovative-citizen-participation-and-new-democratic-institutions_339306da-en.html>).

## S09 — AI risk management

National Institute of Standards and Technology. *Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)*. NIST AI 100-1, 2023. The cited version supplies a process vocabulary for AI risk governance; it is not a certification of a particular system.

2023 framework (<https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf>).

## S10 — AI consciousness

Patrick Butlin and colleagues. *Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness*. 2023. A research report examining theory-derived indicators relevant to possible AI consciousness.

Research report (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708>).

## S11 — Planetary protection

NASA Office of Safety and Mission Assurance. *Planetary Protection*. An institutional introduction to protecting Earth and other solar-system environments from harmful biological contamination associated with exploration. Consult current mission-specific requirements for any actual mission.

Planetary-protection overview (<https://sma.nasa.gov/sma-disciplines/planetary-protection>).

## S12 — SETI detection and response

International Academy of Astronautics SETI Committee. *Declaration of Principles Concerning the Conduct of the Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence*. June 2026 update, reproduced by the SETI Institute. Professional guidance on candidate evidence, verification, communication, preservation, and consultation before a reply.

June 2026 declaration (<https://www.seti.org/research/seti-101/protocols-for-an-eti-signal-detection/>).

## S13 — Indigenous peoples and knowledge

United Nations General Assembly. *United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples*. Resolution 61/295, 2007, particularly Article 31 on cultural heritage, traditional knowledge, and cultural expressions.

UN historical and legal context (<https://legal.un.org/avl/ha/ga_61-295/ga_61-295.html>). Declaration text, Government of Canada (<https://justice.canada.ca/eng/declaration/decl_doc.html>).

## S14 — One Health

Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, United Nations Environment Programme, World Health Organization, and World Organisation for Animal Health. *One Health Joint Plan of Action (2022–2026): Working Together for the Health of Humans, Animals, Plants and the Environment*. 2022.

Plan (<https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240059139>).

## S15 — Open science

UNESCO. *Recommendation on Open Science*. 2021. Principles for openness, participation, and equitable access to scientific knowledge and processes.

UNESCO overview (<https://www.unesco.org/en/open-science/about>). Recommendation (<https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000379949>).

## S16 — Food-system environmental impacts

Joseph Poore and Thomas Nemecek. “Reducing food's environmental impacts through producers and consumers.” *Science* 360, 2018, pages 987–992. DOI: 10.1126/science.aaq0216. Comparative research on variation in food production's environmental impacts and opportunities for reduction.

Oxford Research Archive record (<https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:b0b53649-5e93-4415-bf07-6b0b1227172f>).
