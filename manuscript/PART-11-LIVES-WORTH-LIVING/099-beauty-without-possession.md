# Beauty Without Possession

The view is beautiful until a fence makes it available only to those who can pay. The object is beautiful until its removal leaves the place it came from diminished. Admiration can become a desire to control what should remain encountered on other terms.

Beauty can enrich a life without becoming part of a person's inventory.

**Therefore, create opportunities to encounter beauty through shared access, care, attention, and temporary participation. Protect the places, beings, and makers involved from being reduced to objects of possession.**

## A workable form

Support public landscapes, everyday architecture, performances, gardens, and collections that people can enjoy without purchasing ownership. Include ordinary routes and neighborhoods rather than concentrating beauty in a few celebrated destinations.

Imagine a town restoring access to a riverside while protecting habitat and the privacy of nearby residents. Paths, seating, seasonal restrictions, and maintenance allow encounter without assuming every part of the place must be continuously available to everyone.

Practice forms of temporary relationship: borrowing an object, attending a performance, visiting a place, learning a craft, or helping care for a garden. These can create attachment without requiring exclusive control.

Respect the labor and conditions behind what is admired. Artists, craftspeople, gardeners, and caretakers need support. Shared access does not require their work to be unpaid, nor does appreciation entitle an audience to a person's presence or private life.

## Where it fails

The language of common beauty can justify taking cultural objects or opening sensitive places without consent. Ask who has authority, who bears the burden of access, and what must remain protected.

Beauty can also become an official taste imposed on everyone. Encourage variety, local participation, and room for difficult or unfamiliar work. Public support should not require universal approval of every encounter.

## A first move

Identify one source of beauty people could enjoy more fairly without owning it. Work with its makers or caretakers to improve access while funding care and respecting limits. Notice whether the arrangement deepens attention or simply increases consumption.
