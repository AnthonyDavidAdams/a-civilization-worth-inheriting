# Care Across a Lifetime

A person should not need to become an expert in an institution's internal boundaries to receive care. Yet a change in age, employment, address, or diagnosis can transfer responsibility between systems and leave the person carrying the gap.

Care must follow the life being lived. It must also support the people doing the work, whose exhaustion can become another hidden point of failure.

**Therefore, organize care around continuity, accessibility, and the person's own priorities. Make transitions between services explicit responsibilities with named people accountable for completing them.**

## A workable form

Imagine an older resident returning home after a hospital stay. The discharge document is only one part of the transition. Someone needs to confirm that the home is usable, necessary support is available, and the resident understands whom to contact when something changes. A family member should not silently become the default provider of unlimited unpaid labor.

With the person's permission, coordinate relevant information while protecting unrelated privacy. Provide a comprehensible plan and a point of contact able to resolve gaps. Listen when the person says that the technically available arrangement is intolerable in daily life.

Build services for variation in bodies and capacities. Some people need assistance to exercise choice; others need institutions to stop making decisions for them. Accessible communication, supported decision making, and independent advocacy can make participation more substantive.

Use professional expertise where clinical judgment is required. The institutional proposal here concerns access and continuity; it does not replace individualized care. Measure whether people receive appropriate help and retain agency, not simply whether a referral was issued.

## Where it fails

Integration can become centralization without responsiveness. A single large system may make records easier to share while making mistakes harder to escape. Preserve independent review, alternative providers where feasible, and a clear route to correct inaccurate information.

Care can also become paternalistic. Reducing a person's exposure to every risk may remove activities that make life worth living. Discuss the person's priorities and the consequences of choices rather than assuming protection always means restriction.

## A first move

Examine one transition between services. Identify who remains responsible until the receiving service has actually begun. If the answer is unclear, establish a handoff that the person receiving care can understand and challenge.
