# A Floor Beneath Everyone

The possibility of losing everything changes the meaning of a choice. A person who must accept mistreatment to retain shelter is negotiating under a different condition from someone who can leave. Economic security therefore belongs among the practical foundations of freedom.

A protective floor should cover people whose contribution cannot be captured in wages, people between kinds of work, and people who cannot work. Its purpose is to make a dignified life possible without requiring a favorable judgment of someone's usefulness.

**Therefore, guarantee access to essential conditions of living through arrangements people can understand and use. Make assistance dependable enough that a temporary setback does not become a permanent loss of agency.**

## A workable form

Imagine a resident whose income disappears during a change in employment. A system organized around separate applications might require proof of the same crisis repeatedly while rent and utility bills continue to arrive. A better arrangement offers an accessible point of entry, clear eligibility, prompt decisions, and a way to contest mistakes.

The floor may combine direct services, income support, and shared infrastructure. Its composition should follow actual constraints. Money alone will not create an accessible home where none exists. A nominally free service is not accessible if the journey requires a vehicle a person does not possess.

Define adequacy through lived conditions and public deliberation. Publish how provision is financed and what happens during shortages. Where benefits change with income, examine whether small improvements in earnings can trigger disproportionate losses. Give recipients a voice in designing these transitions.

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights provides a normative foundation for social security and an adequate standard of living; it does not settle the institutional design proposed here. [S04]

## Where it fails

A floor can become a mechanism of supervision. Intrusive monitoring, humiliating interviews, or demands for ideological compliance may make security conditional on submission. Fraud prevention and competent administration matter, but their burdens should be proportionate and open to challenge.

Promises without resources can also become cruel. Publish waiting times and unmet need instead of treating an entitlement on paper as a delivered service.

## A first move

Follow one person through a foreseeable interruption of income or care. Map every delay, repeated document, and gap in provision. Repair the point at which a temporary problem becomes a threat to basic security.
