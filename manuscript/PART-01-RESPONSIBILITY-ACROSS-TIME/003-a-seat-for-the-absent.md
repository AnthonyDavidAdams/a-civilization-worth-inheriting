# A Seat for the Absent

A meeting can be orderly, inclusive among its participants, and still exclude nearly everyone who will bear its consequences. Downstream residents may not know it is happening. Children may have no vote. Animals cannot submit comments. Future inhabitants cannot hire counsel.

Empty chairs do not correct this. Neither does asking the project sponsor to imagine what absent parties might want. Representation requires someone with the means and authority to make a neglected interest consequential.

**Therefore, identify who cannot participate in an important decision and establish independent, accountable representation for their interests. Define the representative's powers narrowly enough to challenge.**

## A workable form

Consider an institution choosing where to build a large facility. In addition to owners and prospective workers, it identifies nearby tenants, people along its supply routes, future maintenance crews, and affected habitats. Some groups need translated information or paid time to participate. Their direct participation is preferable to an outsider claiming to speak for them.

For interests that cannot be represented directly, create a specific mandate. An ecological guardian might investigate habitat effects, commission an independent assessment, question the sponsor, and seek review when agreed limits are breached. A future-residents advocate might scrutinize inherited costs and the possibility of changing the arrangement.

Publish the selection process, funding, conflicts of interest, and conditions for removal. Give representatives access to relevant evidence and enough resources to use it. Require them to distinguish observed harms from their own preferences about a desirable future.

## Where it fails

A representative can become a second source of exclusion. A celebrated advocate may crowd out local knowledge; a permanent guardian may exercise power without ever asking whether the role still helps. Representation should add scrutiny, not create an unanswerable veto over every decision.

Interests also conflict within categories. There is no single view shared by children, future generations, or all species. Record those differences. When a choice harms one group to benefit another, the existence of a representative does not turn the harm into consent.

## A first move

Before the next consequential meeting, draw the boundary of those invited and then the wider boundary of those affected. For each important gap, name a practical remedy: direct participation, an advocate, additional evidence, or a formal opportunity to appeal.
