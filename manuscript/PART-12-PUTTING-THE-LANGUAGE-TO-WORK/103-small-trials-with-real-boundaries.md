# Small Trials With Real Boundaries

A pilot project is called temporary, but people reorganize their lives around it. Data are collected that cannot be recalled. A commercial relationship becomes difficult to leave. Small scale does not automatically make an experiment harmless or reversible.

A useful trial creates learning while keeping its promises and consequences within responsible limits.

**Therefore, test uncertain proposals through bounded trials with clear purposes, informed participation where relevant, stop conditions, and a credible ending. Protect people from bearing serious risks merely because a project is labeled experimental.**

## A workable form

State the question the trial can answer. Specify duration, participants, resources, authority, and what lies outside its scope. Choose observations that could reveal both benefit and harm.

For the public building, a limited program might test opening hours, staffing needs, accessibility, and patterns of use. Tell participants what is temporary, how feedback will affect decisions, and what support will remain if the program ends.

Compare against a meaningful alternative. A trial that merely demonstrates that some people enjoy a new service may leave the important questions unanswered: who is excluded, what work it requires, and whether another arrangement would serve the purpose better.

Assign responsibility for stopping, adapting, or extending the trial. Include a way for participants and neighbors to raise concerns without jeopardizing their access or standing. Preserve useful learning while respecting privacy and agreed limits on information use.

## Where it fails

Pilots can become a way to avoid ordinary accountability. An experiment affecting essential services or rights requires protections appropriate to those stakes, regardless of its size.

They can also remain permanent pilots, supported by exceptional enthusiasm that cannot be sustained. Examine the conditions needed for ordinary operation before claiming that the trial proves scalability.

## A first move

Write a one-page trial agreement naming the learning question, boundaries, responsibilities, stop conditions, and ending plan. Ask an affected person to identify the promise they would rely on most. Make that promise credible before beginning.
