# Independent Eyes

People close to a project see details outsiders miss. They also have reasons to defend it: effort already invested, relationships at stake, and a hope that the work matters. Independent scrutiny can reveal what commitment makes difficult to notice.

Review is most useful when it is informed enough to understand the project and independent enough to challenge it.

**Therefore, invite qualified scrutiny from people who can examine consequential claims without depending on the project's success. Give them access, a clear remit, and a route for findings to affect decisions.**

## A workable form

Choose reviewers for relevant expertise, practical understanding, and freedom from conflicts that would compromise judgment. Independence is a relationship to the decision, not a claim that anyone has no perspective.

For the public building, reviewers might examine accessibility, finances, maintenance, participation, and effects on neighbors. They speak with users and staff directly, including people who stopped attending or never found a way in.

Agree on the questions while allowing reviewers to identify consequential issues outside the initial brief. Publish methods, limitations, and material findings in an understandable form. Provide a fair opportunity for the project team to respond without granting it control over the conclusions.

Connect review to authority. Someone must decide what happens next, explain that decision, and follow up on required changes. An independent report that no decision-maker needs to acknowledge offers limited protection.

## Where it fails

Review can become a ritual of approval purchased from a familiar provider. Disclose funding, selection, scope, and restrictions on access. Rotate perspectives where useful without sacrificing continuity of knowledge.

Outsiders can also miss local realities or impose inappropriate models. Combine independence with listening, direct observation, and humility about context. The purpose is better judgment, not automatic superiority of distant expertise.

## A first move

Identify the claim your project most needs an outsider to believe. Invite a qualified reviewer to test it, provide the evidence they reasonably need, and agree in advance how an unfavorable finding would change the next decision.
