# The patterns at a glance

## Part I  Responsibility across time

**1. Leave Room for the Future** — Preserve the conditions and freedom for descendants to choose.

**2. Enough for the Living** — Meet present needs while building capacity for later generations.

**3. A Seat for the Absent** — Give unrepresented interests independent advocates with bounded powers.

**4. A Floor Beneath Everyone** — Protect basic security from fortune, productivity, and political favor.

**5. Many Ways to Flourish** — Allow different good lives within shared protections against harm.

**6. Proportionate Caution** — Match safeguards to the scale and reversibility of possible damage.

**7. The Right to Reconsider** — Build review, appeal, amendment, and exit into lasting arrangements.

**8. Decisions at the Right Scale** — Keep authority close while governing consequences that cross borders.

**9. Keep the Capacity to Repair** — Preserve the skills and resources needed to correct mistakes.

## Part II  Becoming human together

**10. Safe Beginnings** — Make early life secure without prescribing a single family form.

**11. A Village Around Every Child** — Support caregivers and reproductive freedom through reliable public provision.

**12. The Skill of Relationship** — Practice listening, boundaries, conflict, commitment, and repair.

**13. Consent as a Daily Practice** — Make refusal intelligible, consequential, and safe.

**14. A Common Table** — Create recurring places to share food, attention, and responsibility.

**15. A Place to Be Alone** — Protect privacy and solitude within a connected life.

**16. Care Across a Lifetime** — Organize health, disability support, and aging around actual needs.

**17. Work With Meaning** — Offer contribution, mastery, and recognition beyond economic necessity.

**18. A Dignified Death** — Support choice, companionship, and relief at the end of life.

## Part III  The living planet

**19. Watersheds as Commons** — Coordinate water responsibilities across property and political boundaries.

**20. Room for Wildness** — Protect habitat where other species can pursue their own lives.

**21. Connected Habitats** — Let living populations move as seasons and climates change.

**22. Animals With Interests** — Consider individual suffering and agency alongside species survival.

**23. Food Without Cruelty** — Build nourishing food systems that move beyond animal confinement and slaughter.

**24. Soil as an Inheritance** — Restore fertility, living structure, and the capacity to retain water.

**25. Oceans With Guardians** — Assign enforceable duties for ecosystems beyond easy public scrutiny.

**26. End Pollution at the Source** — Prevent persistent harm and fund cleanup where prevention fails.

**27. Guardians With Duties** — Make ecological representatives accountable for evidence, conflicts, and results.

## Part IV  Places and material life

**28. Settlements That Belong to Place** — Design with local ecology, climate, history, and inhabitants.

**29. Housing That Can Change** — Let homes adapt to different bodies, households, and uses.

**30. Clean Energy in Many Hands** — Combine reliable supply with public accountability and distributed capability.

**31. Materials That Can Return** — Design for reuse while accounting for losses and extraction.

**32. Pay for Maintenance First** — Fund upkeep, repair, replacement, and retirement before expansion.

**33. Beauty in Ordinary Places** — Bring proportion, craft, sensory pleasure, and living things into daily life.

**34. Public Space Without a Purchase** — Provide places to gather without having to buy access.

**35. Water Where Life Needs It** — Coordinate drinking water, sanitation, flood protection, and ecological flows.

**36. Enough Local Capacity** — Retain essential capabilities while participating in wider networks.

## Part V  An economy that supports life

**37. An Economy of Enough** — Secure sufficiency and make room for ambition without compulsory consumption.

**38. Account for the Full Cost** — Expose harms shifted onto strangers, animals, and future inhabitants.

**39. Share the Gains of Invention** — Broaden participation in the wealth created by improving technology.

**40. Common Wealth Dividends** — Return a share of jointly created value to the public.

**41. Land With Lasting Duties** — Connect landholding to stewardship, access, and responsibility for displacement.

**42. Ownership With Obligations** — Match control and reward with liability and public duties.

**43. Pay for Care** — Recognize and support labor that sustains people beyond formal markets.

**44. Finance That Can Wait** — Fund long projects without giving creditors permanent political control.

**45. A Fair Transition** — Support affected workers and communities as harmful systems are retired.

## Part VI  Power that remains answerable

**46. Power Must Explain Itself** — Require reasons, disclosure, review, and enforceable limits.

**47. Citizens Who Deliberate** — Combine representative institutions with informed, inclusive public judgment.

**48. Rights That Majorities Cannot Remove** — Protect basic freedoms and equal standing against popularity contests.

**49. The Right to Exit and Belong** — Allow departure without making displacement the price of dissent.

**50. Commons at Every Scale** — Match shared resources with usable rules and accountable stewardship.

**51. Treaties for Shared Hazards** — Govern risks that no community can contain by itself.

**52. Peace as a Maintained Practice** — Invest in prevention, negotiation, verification, and repair after violence.

**53. Emergency Powers That End** — Require deadlines, independent review, and restoration of ordinary authority.

**54. Constitutions That Can Learn** — Allow legitimate revision while protecting people during political change.

## Part VII  A civilization that can know

**55. Shared Reality** — Support reliable public inquiry without installing an official monopoly on truth.

**56. Show the Work** — Make consequential claims traceable to methods, evidence, and uncertainty.

**57. Keep Dissent Safe** — Protect challenges to authority, including scientific and cultural authority.

**58. Learning Across a Lifetime** — Make education available across age, circumstance, and changing capabilities.

**59. Libraries That Survive** — Preserve knowledge through distributed copies, translation, and usable formats.

**60. Local Knowledge With Consent** — Learn with communities while respecting attribution, refusal, and control.

**61. Public Interest Science** — Sustain independent research into neglected needs and shared dangers.

**62. Memory of Harm** — Preserve testimony and accountability without making inherited hostility compulsory.

**63. Measure What Matters** — Use plural indicators, distributional evidence, and direct lived experience.

## Part VIII  Living with artificial minds

**64. Delegated Intelligence** — Give systems explicit purposes, bounded authority, and answerable operators.

**65. Auditable Power** — Make consequential automated decisions inspectable and open to meaningful appeal.

**66. Capability With Containment** — Tie access to dangerous capabilities to demonstrated control and oversight.

**67. Freedom From Machine Persuasion** — Protect attention, privacy, and consent against manipulative optimization.

**68. Human Skills Worth Keeping** — Retain competence wherever dependence could destroy meaningful choice.

**69. A Commons of Useful Tools** — Share benefits broadly while governing dangerous capabilities proportionately.

**70. Uncertain Minds Deserve Care** — Reduce plausible suffering without pretending consciousness has been settled.

**71. Personhood Beyond Resemblance** — Evaluate possible interests without confusing fluent performance with experience.

**72. No Irreversible Surrender** — Preserve accountable control and workable alternatives to concentrated machine power.

## Part IX  Continuity through disruption

**73. Diverse Systems** — Avoid dependence on one supplier, model, crop, or institutional design.

**74. Failure That Stays Local** — Prevent breakdowns from spreading through every essential network.

**75. Public Health for All Species** — Coordinate prevention across human, animal, and ecological health.

**76. Reserves Before Scarcity** — Maintain accessible buffers of essentials with transparent release rules.

**77. Practice the Emergency** — Rehearse real responsibilities without turning preparation into public intimidation.

**78. Recovery Before Disaster** — Establish restoration plans, mutual aid, and fair claims in advance.

**79. A Civilization That Can Reboot** — Preserve usable tools and knowledge across severe infrastructure loss.

**80. Institutions With Successors** — Transfer skills and authority before founders become indispensable.

**81. Remember the Near Misses** — Turn narrowly avoided failures into shared, revisable knowledge.

## Part X  Neighbors in the cosmos

**82. Earth Is Not Disposable** — Preserve our living home as capabilities beyond it expand.

**83. Space as a Shared Responsibility** — Govern debris, resources, access, and responsibilities across borders.

**84. Worlds With Their Own Claims** — Investigate before disturbing places that may harbor unfamiliar life.

**85. Settlements That Can Refuse** — Protect consent where dependence on life support magnifies coercion.

**86. Listen Before Sending** — Distinguish observation from transmissions that expose others to consequences.

**87. Confirm Before Announcing** — Verify extraordinary evidence through independent, transparent examination.

**88. No One Speaks for Earth** — Require legitimate, plural authorization for acts claiming planetary representation.

**89. Contact Without Contamination** — Control biological, ecological, and informational hazards in both directions.

**90. The Right to Remain Unknown** — Respect refusal, privacy, and limits to disclosure across civilizations.

## Part XI  Lives worth living

**91. Time That Is Your Own** — Protect leisure and autonomy as productivity and lifespans change.

**92. A Living Culture** — Support creation, transmission, adaptation, and cultural self-determination.

**93. Rituals for Change** — Mark birth, commitment, transition, loss, and collective renewal.

**94. Wonder Without Compulsion** — Protect spiritual inquiry alongside freedom from imposed belief.

**95. Games Worth Playing** — Create challenge, mastery, delight, and cooperation without manufactured desperation.

**96. Hospitality Across Difference** — Welcome encounter while respecting boundaries and reciprocal responsibilities.

**97. Freedom to Be Strange** — Leave room for eccentricity, experimentation, and harmless refusal.

**98. Grief With Somewhere to Go** — Give private and collective loss companionship, expression, and time.

**99. Beauty Without Possession** — Expand opportunities to enjoy what need not be individually owned.

## Part XII  Putting the language to work

**100. Begin With a Place** — Choose a real place, its inhabitants, and a tractable need.

**101. Map Who Bears the Cost** — Trace benefits, burdens, dependencies, and missing voices.

**102. Assemble a Working Language** — Connect the patterns needed for one coherent intervention.

**103. Small Trials With Real Boundaries** — Test safely with consent and limits on exposure.

**104. Independent Eyes** — Invite reviewers who can challenge incentives and conclusions.

**105. Publish the Failures** — Share negative results while protecting personal privacy.

**106. Make Better Practices Easier** — Remove obstacles in defaults, financing, training, and rules.

**107. Return and Reconsider** — Revisit consequences, repair harms, and revise or retire.

**108. Keep the Language Open** — Preserve attribution, competing versions, and freedom to improve.
