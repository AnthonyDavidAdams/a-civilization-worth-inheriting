# Guardians With Duties

Giving a river a representative raises an unavoidable question: who holds the representative accountable? A guardian can bring neglected interests into a decision, but can also turn those interests into a source of personal authority.

The role should be designed around responsibilities that others can inspect. Reverence for the thing represented does not make the representative infallible.

**Therefore, give guardians explicit mandates, independent resources, obligations to disclose evidence and conflicts, and procedures for challenge, review, and replacement.**

## A workable form

Imagine a watershed establishing an ecological guardian. The mandate identifies the functions and interests the guardian must consider, the decisions in which they can intervene, and the limits of their authority. It distinguishes powers to investigate, require a response, initiate review, and stop an activity under specified conditions.

Select guardians through a process that includes relevant knowledge and affected communities. Provide stable funding that does not depend on pleasing the project sponsor. Require public reasons for consequential actions, with appropriate protection for sensitive information such as vulnerable habitat locations.

Evaluate performance through several forms of evidence. Ecological conditions matter, but so do the quality of investigation, responsiveness to new knowledge, and fairness of procedure. A guardian should not be punished simply because external conditions worsen despite responsible work.

Provide a route for residents and independent experts to challenge errors. Replacement should be possible for misconduct or sustained failure, with safeguards against removal merely because the guardian has become inconvenient to a powerful actor.

## Where it fails

Broad moral language can hide an undefined mandate. If a guardian may intervene in everything without explaining a relevant connection, the office becomes difficult to distinguish from general political authority.

The reverse failure is a ceremonial role with no access, staff, or power to obtain a response. A title cannot compensate for institutional weakness. Match authority and resources to specific duties, then review whether the arrangement brings previously ignored consequences into real decisions.

## A first move

Draft a one-page mandate for an existing or proposed representative. Include whom or what they represent, their evidence obligations, powers, funding, and removal procedure. Ask both supporters and likely critics where it could be abused or rendered ineffective.
