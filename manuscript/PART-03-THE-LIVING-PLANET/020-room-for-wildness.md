# Room for Wildness

A landscape can retain its green appearance while leaving other species little room to live. A narrow decorative margin beside continuous development may satisfy a visual preference without supporting the activities of the beings displaced.

Wildness means allowing ecological processes and other lives substantial space beyond continuous human direction. It does not imply that a place has no human history or that protecting it requires removing its existing stewards.

**Therefore, protect and restore sufficient, connected habitat for other species to pursue their lives. Establish durable protection through legitimate relationships with the people who inhabit and care for the place.**

## A workable form

Consider a region deciding which land to develop. Begin with ecological function, habitat quality, and connections, as well as the needs and rights of resident communities. Count the combined effects of many small decisions. A series of individually modest losses can undermine the remaining whole.

The IPBES global assessment describes extensive human-driven changes to biodiversity and ecosystems. It provides a scientific basis for taking cumulative loss seriously; it does not resolve every local choice among forms of protection. [S06]

Secure protection through arrangements whose responsibilities and funding survive a change in leadership. Work with existing stewards on access, restoration, and the conditions under which intervention is appropriate. Keep monitoring proportionate and avoid turning every habitat into a public attraction that defeats its purpose.

Allow ecological knowledge to change the design. A boundary that looks elegant on a map may divide an essential movement route. Protection should consider what happens outside it, including pollution, noise, extraction, and changing climate.

## Where it fails

Conservation can become a justification for displacement or a luxury amenity that raises nearby housing costs. Protect people from those effects while preserving the ecological purpose. Do not treat consultation after a plan is fixed as meaningful participation.

Wildness is also compatible with suffering. Protecting a functioning ecosystem does not answer every question about helping an injured animal. Keep ecological integrity and individual welfare visible as related but distinct concerns.

## A first move

Identify a habitat under pressure and learn which functions it actually supports. Work with local stewards and relevant expertise to protect the feature most at risk, including its connections, rather than simply preserving a convenient acreage.
