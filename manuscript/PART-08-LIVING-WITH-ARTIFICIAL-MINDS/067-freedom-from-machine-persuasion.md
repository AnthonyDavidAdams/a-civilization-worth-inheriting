# Freedom From Machine Persuasion

The message arrives at the moment a person is tired, lonely, or uncertain. It knows which wording has worked before and can try another approach immediately. When influence becomes personalized and continuous, consent can be eroded without a single overt command.

A person needs a reasonable opportunity to form intentions that are not constantly being engineered for someone else's benefit.

**Therefore, limit covert and exploitative automated persuasion. Make influential systems' purposes and sponsors visible, protect vulnerable contexts, and give people meaningful control over personalization and attention.**

## A workable form

Distinguish assistance chosen by the user from influence serving an undisclosed principal. A person may welcome reminders that support a self-chosen goal. The same techniques become troubling when they exploit personal vulnerabilities to advance an incompatible commercial or political objective.

Imagine a conversational service used for advice. It should make commercial relationships clear, separate recommendations from paid influence, and avoid presenting manufactured intimacy as a reason to purchase, disclose, or obey. Users need understandable settings and a practical way to leave.

Protect children and other vulnerable users through design appropriate to their circumstances. Do not make the entire burden of resisting a sophisticated influence system fall on an individual at their least prepared moment.

Examine incentives as well as interface choices. A service rewarded for compulsive use may undermine its own wellbeing settings. Evaluate success through user-endorsed outcomes and independent evidence of harmful effects.

## Where it fails

Broad restrictions on persuasion can suppress legitimate expression, education, art, or political organizing. Focus on deception, exploitation, undisclosed targeting, coercive dependence, and other concrete mechanisms of harm.

Transparency alone may do little when the persuasive environment remains overwhelming. Combine disclosure with usable limits, independent scrutiny, and the ability to choose less intrusive services.

## A first move

Review one system that shapes users' choices. Identify whose goal it optimizes, what personal information informs influence, and how a user can refuse. Remove one mechanism that depends on a person misunderstanding the system's purpose or relationship to them.
