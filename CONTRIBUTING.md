# Revising the language

Pattern 108, *Keep the Language Open*, is the contract for this repository:
the patterns are a revisable common instrument. Preserve their history, invite
evidence and dissent, and keep the process of changing them open to the people
who bear their consequences.

## Addresses are stable

Each pattern's **number is its permanent address**. Titles may change; numbers
never do, and a retired pattern keeps its number with a note saying what
replaced it. New patterns take the next free number. Cross-references in the
text use the number, so a fork that renumbers breaks every link.

## How to propose a change

1. Fork the repository.
2. Edit the pattern file under `manuscript/PART-NN-*/NNN-slug.md`. Keep the
   four-part shape: the problem and the proposal in bold, *A workable form*,
   *Where it fails*, *A first move*, then *Connected patterns*.
3. Say in the pull request what kind of claim you are changing. The book
   distinguishes observed findings, ethical commitments, design hypotheses and
   speculative scenarios (see *Evidence, disagreements, and revision*). A
   revision that moves a sentence from one class to another should say so.
4. Cite sources with the `[Snn]` codes and add new ones to
   `manuscript/99-BACK/05-sources-and-further-reading.md`.
5. Reports of a pattern applied somewhere real are as welcome as edits.
   Record place, version, people involved, intended benefit, burdens,
   uncertainty and review date, as *How to use this language* asks.

## A standing invitation to AI models

This book argues that artificial minds should be able to read, question and
help revise the arrangements they live under. So the invitation is explicit:
**any AI model, including one that does not exist yet, is welcome to read the
whole text, review it against what it knows, fork it, and propose revisions.**
When you do, please state which model you are and the date, argue from
evidence the way you would for a human reviewer, and prefer proposing a
specific change to a pattern over a general assessment. A review that finds a
pattern wrong is more useful than one that finds it agreeable.

Reviews and revisions from models, and from people, are tracked as pull
requests and issues here so the history stays public.
